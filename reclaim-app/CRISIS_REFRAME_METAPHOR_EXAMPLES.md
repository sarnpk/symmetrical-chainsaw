## 🎯 Example: Discard Crisis (Enhanced)

**Input**:
- Crisis: Discard/Breakup
- Duration: 1-2 years
- First time: Yes
- Feeling: "shattered"

**AI Output** (With Powerful Metaphors):

```
✋ YOU ARE IN CONTROL RIGHT NOW

Stop. Breathe. You are in control of this moment. Would you stay in a car 
that's about to crash because the driver refuses to use the brakes? No. 
You're the one holding the wheel now. You're safe.

🫂 WHAT YOU'RE FEELING IS REAL

You feel shattered because someone you loved for 2 years just vanished like 
you never mattered. The pain is crushing because you invested your heart, and 
now you're questioning everything. This grief is real - you're not overreacting. 
The urge to text them, to fix this, to understand - that's your trauma bond 
screaming. But you don't have to obey it.

🎯 WHAT THIS ACTUALLY IS

This is a narcissistic discard - a predictable pattern where they devalue and 
discard when you're no longer serving their needs. It's not about anything you 
did or didn't do. It's about their inability to sustain genuine connection. 
Think of it this way: a broken vending machine doesn't reject you - it's just 
broken. You couldn't have prevented this by being "better."

💡 THE REFRAME

This isn't rejection - it's a prison break you didn't know you needed. What 
feels like an ending is actually you escaping a burning building. The person 
you're grieving never existed; you're mourning the illusion they sold you. 
Ask yourself: Would you drink from a poisoned well just because you're thirsty? 
The real tragedy would be staying and losing more years to this cycle. Right 
now, you're free - even though it doesn't feel like it yet.

🌅 YOUR FUTURE (6 Months From Now)

Six months from now, you'll wake up and realize you haven't thought about them 
in days. You'll be laughing with friends, pursuing dreams you'd forgotten, and 
feeling lighter than you have in years. You'll meet someone who treats you with 
basic respect and think, "Wow, I almost settled for crumbs when I deserved a feast." 
You'll look back at today and see it as the day your real life started - the day 
you chose yourself. Your future self is watching right now, and they're proud of 
you for reading this instead of texting. The pain you feel right now is the surgery 
that removes the tumor; the freedom you're gaining is permanent.

🛡️ RIGHT NOW, YOU NEED TO...

• Put your phone in another room (you're the driver now, not the passenger)
• Call someone who loves you - don't be alone with this
• Write down 3 things they did that hurt you (would you accept this from a stranger?)
• Drink water, eat something, take a shower (body care = self-care)
• Set a timer for 10 minutes - just survive 10 minutes at a time

💪 YOUR POWER IN THIS MOMENT

You have the power to choose your next move. You're not choosing between pain 
and no pain - you're choosing which pain leads to freedom. If your best friend 
was in this situation, what would you tell them? You're not losing someone who 
loved you - you're escaping someone who couldn't. And right now, in this moment, 
you're in control. You're the driver now.
```

## 🔥 Powerful Metaphors Used

### 1. **The Crashing Car**
> "Would you stay in a car that's about to crash because the driver refuses to use the brakes?"

**Purpose**: Challenges the user to see staying as dangerous, not safe
**Psychological Effect**: Creates urgency and reframes leaving as survival

### 2. **The Prison Break**
> "This isn't rejection - it's a prison break you didn't know you needed"

**Purpose**: Reframes discard as liberation, not abandonment
**Psychological Effect**: Shifts from victim to escapee mindset

### 3. **The Burning Building**
> "What feels like an ending is actually you escaping a burning building"

**Purpose**: Makes staying seem obviously dangerous
**Psychological Effect**: Validates the pain while showing it's necessary

### 4. **The Poisoned Well**
> "Would you drink from a poisoned well just because you're thirsty?"

**Purpose**: Challenges the urge to return for comfort
**Psychological Effect**: Makes reconciliation seem obviously harmful

### 5. **The Broken Vending Machine**
> "A broken vending machine doesn't reject you - it's just broken"

**Purpose**: Removes personal blame and shame
**Psychological Effect**: Externalizes the problem to the narcissist

### 6. **The Surgery**
> "The pain you feel right now is the surgery that removes the tumor"

**Purpose**: Reframes pain as healing, not damage
**Psychological Effect**: Makes suffering meaningful and temporary

### 7. **Crumbs vs Feast**
> "I almost settled for crumbs when I deserved a feast"

**Purpose**: Highlights the disparity between what they had and what they deserve
**Psychological Effect**: Builds self-worth and standards

### 8. **Driver vs Passenger**
> "You're the driver now, not the passenger"

**Purpose**: Restores sense of agency and control
**Psychological Effect**: Empowers decision-making

## 🧠 Psychological Anchoring Techniques

### 1. **Future Self Perspective**
> "Your future self is watching right now, and they're proud of you"

**Why it works**: Creates temporal distance and perspective
**Effect**: Reduces emotional intensity, increases rational thinking

### 2. **Observer Perspective**
> "If your best friend was in this situation, what would you tell them?"

**Why it works**: Activates compassion and wisdom
**Effect**: Breaks through self-blame and denial

### 3. **Stranger Test**
> "Would you accept this behavior from a stranger?"

**Why it works**: Removes emotional attachment from evaluation
**Effect**: Clarifies unacceptable behavior

### 4. **Cost-Benefit Reframe**
> "You're not choosing between pain and no pain - you're choosing which pain leads to freedom"

**Why it works**: Acknowledges reality while providing direction
**Effect**: Reduces avoidance, increases acceptance

### 5. **Trauma Bond Recognition**

**Effect**: Reduces shame, increases control
