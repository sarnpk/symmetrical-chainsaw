🧠 Mind Reset - User Guide

What is Mind Reset?

Mind Reset is your toolkit for reframing negative thought patterns, practicing positive affirmations, and using breathing exercises to calm your nervous system and shift your mindset. When you're stuck in trauma loops, anxiety spirals, or negative self-talk, these tools help you reset.

The Three Tools

💡 Thought Patterns (Cognitive Reframing)

Recognize and challenge distorted thinking patterns that keep you stuck.

💗 Affirmations

Rewire your brain with positive, validating statements designed for abuse survivors.

🌬️ Breathing Exercises

Calm your nervous system with guided breathing patterns.

Tool 1: Thought Patterns

What Are Cognitive Distortions?

Cognitive distortions are automatic negative thought patterns that your brain falls into, especially after trauma. They're not based on reality - they're based on fear, past experiences, and survival instincts.

The 5 Common Patterns

1. Catastrophizing

What it is: Assuming the worst possible outcome will happen

Example thought: "If I leave this relationship, I'll never find love again and I'll be alone forever."

Reframe: "Ending an unhealthy relationship opens up space for healthier connections. Many people find fulfilling relationships after difficult ones."

2. Self-Blame

What it is: Taking responsibility for things outside your control

Example thought: "It's my fault they got angry. I should have known better than to bring that up."

Reframe: "I am not responsible for someone else's emotional reactions. Their anger is their choice and responsibility."

3. All-or-Nothing Thinking

What it is: Seeing situations in black and white with no middle ground

Example thought: "I'm either perfect or I'm a complete failure. There's no in-between."

Reframe: "Life exists in shades of gray. I can make mistakes and still be a good person who is learning and growing."

4. Mind Reading

What it is: Assuming you know what others are thinking without evidence

Example thought: "They're quiet today, so they must be angry with me about something I did."

Reframe: "I don't know what others are thinking unless they tell me. There could be many reasons for their behavior that have nothing to do with me."

5. Fortune Telling

What it is: Predicting negative outcomes without evidence

Example thought: "I know this therapy won't work for me. Nothing ever helps."

Reframe: "I can't predict the future. Each new approach is an opportunity to learn and grow, regardless of past experiences."

How to Use the AI Reframe Tool

  1. Catch the thought: Notice when you're having a negative thought
  2. Write it down: Type it exactly as it appears in your mind
  3. Click "Reframe My Thought": AI will generate a healthier perspective
  4. Read the reframe: Does it feel more balanced? More true?
  5. Practice it: Repeat the reframed thought when the old one appears
💡 Usage Limits:
• Foundation: No AI reframes (manual patterns only)
• Recovery: 100 AI reframes per month
• Empowerment: Unlimited AI reframes

Your reframe history is saved so you can review past sessions.

Tool 2: Affirmations

Why Affirmations Work

After abuse, your brain has been programmed with negative beliefs about yourself. Affirmations help rewire those neural pathways by repeatedly exposing your brain to positive, true statements.

How to Use Affirmations

  • Choose a playlist: Self-worth, Boundaries, Healing, etc.
  • Read slowly: Don't rush. Let each word sink in.
  • Repeat out loud: Hearing yourself say it makes it more powerful
  • Use arrow keys: Navigate with keyboard (← →) or click arrows
  • Favorite the ones that resonate: Click the heart icon
  • Auto-play mode: Let affirmations cycle automatically (5 seconds each)
Example Affirmations:
• "I am worthy of love and respect"
• "My feelings and experiences are valid"
• "I have the strength to heal and grow"
• "I deserve healthy relationships"
• "I trust my own perceptions and instincts"
• "I am not responsible for others' actions"

When to Use Affirmations

  • Morning routine: Start your day with positive self-talk
  • Before difficult situations: Remind yourself of your worth
  • After contact with abuser: Counter their negative messages
  • When doubting yourself: Replace self-criticism with self-compassion
  • Before bed: End the day with kindness toward yourself
⚠️ "But I don't believe it yet..."
That's normal. You don't have to believe affirmations for them to work. Your brain is learning a new language. Keep repeating them even when they feel fake. Over time (weeks to months), they'll start to feel true.

Tool 3: Breathing Exercises

Why Breathing Matters

When you're anxious, triggered, or in fight-or-flight mode, your breathing becomes shallow and fast. This signals danger to your brain, which makes anxiety worse. Controlled breathing tells your nervous system it's safe, activating your parasympathetic (rest and digest) system.

The 4 Breathing Patterns

1. Box Breathing (4-4-4-4)

Best for: Acute anxiety, panic attacks, grounding

  • Inhale for 4 seconds
  • Hold for 4 seconds
  • Exhale for 4 seconds
  • Hold for 4 seconds
  • Repeat

2. 4-7-8 Breathing

Best for: Falling asleep, deep relaxation

  • Inhale for 4 seconds
  • Hold for 7 seconds
  • Exhale for 8 seconds
  • Repeat

3. Equal Breathing (5-5)

Best for: Focus, concentration, calm energy

  • Inhale for 5 seconds
  • Exhale for 5 seconds
  • Repeat

4. Coherent Breathing (5-5)

Best for: Heart rate variability, long-term stress reduction

  • Inhale for 5 seconds
  • Exhale for 5 seconds
  • Repeat for 10-20 minutes

How to Use the Breathing Tool

  1. Choose a pattern: Pick based on what you need (calm, sleep, focus)
  2. Set duration: Start with 2-3 minutes, work up to 5-10
  3. Click "Start": Follow the on-screen timer
  4. Breathe with the rhythm: Inhale when it says inhale, hold when it says hold
  5. Don't force it: If you feel dizzy, slow down or stop
Pro Tip: Close your eyes while breathing. Put one hand on your chest and one on your belly. Feel your belly expand on the inhale (diaphragmatic breathing). This activates the vagus nerve, which calms your nervous system even more.

When to Use Each Tool

Situation Tool to Use
Stuck in negative thought loop Thought Patterns - Reframe the thought
Feeling worthless or unlovable Affirmations - Self-worth playlist
Panic attack or acute anxiety Breathing - Box breathing
Can't fall asleep Breathing - 4-7-8 breathing
Morning routine / starting the day Affirmations - Any playlist
After contact with abuser All three - Breathe first, then reframe, then affirm
Feeling triggered but don't know why Breathing - Calm your body first, then investigate

Building a Daily Practice

Morning Routine (5 minutes)

  1. Breathing: 2 minutes of equal breathing to wake up calmly
  2. Affirmations: 3 minutes cycling through your favorite playlist

Midday Reset (3 minutes)

  1. Thought Pattern Check: Notice any negative thoughts from the morning
  2. Quick Reframe: Use AI or manual reframe

Evening Wind-Down (10 minutes)

  1. Breathing: 5 minutes of 4-7-8 breathing
  2. Affirmations: 5 minutes of healing/self-compassion playlist

Tips for Success

  • Start small: 2-3 minutes per day is better than nothing
  • Be consistent: Same time each day builds the habit
  • Don't judge yourself: Some days you'll believe the affirmations, some days you won't. Keep going.
  • Track your progress: Notice how you feel after 1 week, 2 weeks, 1 month
  • Combine tools: Breathe first to calm your body, then reframe thoughts, then affirm your worth
  • Use during triggers: The more you practice when calm, the easier it is to use when triggered

Remember

Your brain is not broken - it's protecting you. These negative thought patterns, anxiety responses, and self-doubt are survival mechanisms that helped you cope with abuse. Now you're teaching your brain new patterns. Be patient with yourself. Healing takes time, but every reframe, every affirmation, every breath is rewiring your nervous system toward safety and peace.
