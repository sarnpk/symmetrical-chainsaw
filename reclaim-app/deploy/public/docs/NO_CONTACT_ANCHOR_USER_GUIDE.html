What Is No Contact?

No contact is the practice of completely disconnecting from an abusive or toxic ex-partner. It breaks the trauma bond and allows your nervous system to begin healing. The first weeks are the hardest - this tool is designed to keep you anchored through them.

Why It Hurts (And Why That's Normal): The anxiety, panic, and longing you feel are withdrawal symptoms from a trauma bond - not proof you made a mistake. Like an addiction, every contact re-triggers the dependency. The feeling is temporary and will pass like a wave. This tool gives you a count of how many days you've stayed strong, a place to log urges instead of acting on them, and your reasons for leaving when you need them most.

Before You Begin: What You'll Need

  • No Contact Start Date - the day you began (or will begin) no contact
  • Toxic Memories - documentation of what they did (used for your "Why I Left" list)
  • Safety Plan - your safe contacts and crisis resources

Getting Started (2 Minutes)

1 Set Your Start Date

Open No Contact Anchor from your sidebar or dashboard. On first visit you'll be prompted to enter the date your no contact period began. This powers your day counter, so pick the real date if possible.

2 Print Your Reasons for Leaving

On the No Contact Anchor page, in the "Why I Left" section, review your toxic memories. If the list is empty, visit the Toxic Memory Journal first to document what happened. These are your anchor - read them whenever the "good times" start rewriting history.

3 Commit to a Support Network

Add safe contacts and crisis resources in your Safety Plan. When an urge hits, you'll want a person to reach out to instead of them.

Logging an Urge (The Core Practice)

When you feel the pull to contact them, don't fight the feeling silently - log it instead. This does three things: it delays the impulse, it reveals the pattern, and it builds a record that the intensity is fading.

  1. Tap Log Urge.
  2. Set the urge intensity from 1 to 10.
  3. Select the symptoms you're experiencing (anxiety, panic, longing, anger, relief, confusion, sadness - or your own).
  4. Describe what triggered the urge.
  5. Describe how you resisted it.
  6. Tap Log Urge and reward yourself - you just won a round.
Track the Trend: Logging every urge lets you watch the Urge Intensity Trend decline over time. On day one an urge might be a 9; by week three it's often a 4. That chart is hard evidence that healing is happening.

The "Before You Contact Them" Moment

The red Before You Contact Them - READ THIS button exists for the hardest moments. When you open it, the app will:

  • Remind you that this feeling is trauma bond withdrawal, not love or a sign of failure.
  • Show how many days you've stayed strong and urge you not to break the streak.
  • Display your "Why I Left" list - the reality you've forgotten in the moment.
  • Remind you the "good times" were part of the manipulation cycle.
  • Offer to call a safe contact or log the urge instead.
If you're in immediate danger: use your Safety Plan or call local emergency services. The app is a support tool, not a substitute for crisis care.

Frequently Asked Questions

I slipped and contacted them. Do I reset the counter?

That's up to you, and there is no shame either way. Many people reset on a slip because it represents a new commitment. Others keep the original start date. The point isn't the number - it's that you keep going. Use Update Start Date if you decide to reset.

Why does the app ask for my start date?

Your start date powers the day counter and milestone tracking, so you can see your progress at a glance. It's stored only in your account.

How do I add more reasons to my "Why I Left" list?

Document incidents in the Toxic Memory Journal. The No Contact Anchor pulls from your most recent entries automatically.

Where do my urge logs go?

They're saved privately to your account and shown in the trend chart and history on this page.
