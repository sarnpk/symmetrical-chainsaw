Urge Surfing Guide

The Truth: No urge lasts forever. Your brain tells you the urge will build forever or break you—that's the lie. Urges peak and pass like waves.

What is Urge Surfing?

Urge surfing is a mindfulness technique that helps you ride out intense urges without giving in. Instead of fighting the urge or acting on it, you observe it like a wave—it rises, peaks, and naturally falls.

💡 Key Insight: The average urge peaks in 15-30 minutes. If you can surf that wave, the intensity will naturally decrease.

How to Use This Feature

  1. Start Surfing: When you feel an urge, tap "Start Surfing" and select your urge type.
  2. Enable Voice Guidance: Toggle voice ON to hear a calming script specific to your urge.
  3. Start the Timer: Begin timing your surf session. The timer helps you see that urges are temporary.
  4. Track Intensity: Rate your urge intensity at the start, peak, and end to visualize the wave.
  5. Record Success: Note whether you surfed successfully or gave in—no judgment, just data.

Urge Types & Scripts

🔥 Lashing Out & Anger

Trigger: Feeling unheard, disrespected, unloved, or overstimulated

Body Sensations: Heat in face, racing heart, pressure behind eyes

"Whoa. I am at a level 10. My face feels hot. The urge to attack is huge. This is just energy moving through me. It is important energy, but it needs to be packaged better before it's delivered. I will not speak while the wave is this high. I'm going to unclench my hands and ride this wave until it cools down to a simmer. I can understand this anger and know it's allowed to be here, without acting on it."

💳 Impulsive Spending

Trigger: Insecurity, boredom, promise of dopamine

Body Sensations: Rush of excitement, tunnel vision, itch to click

"I'm noticing that my brain is seeking a rush of dopamine. My brain thinks buying this will fix my mood. It's right, but only for about 30 seconds. Then dread and guilt will kick in. The urgency to click is high, but I'm going to close the tab. I will surf this 'wanting' sensation. It feels like an itch, but an itch is just uncomfortable. It doesn't kill you if you don't scratch it."

💔 Anxious Attachment (Ex/Partner/Children)

Trigger: Uncertainty, fear of abandonment

Body Sensations: Knot in stomach, shallow breath, frantic grasping energy

"This is an attachment spike. I feel terrified right now. My brain is telling me to text them to make the anxiety stop. But texting is a compulsion, not a connection. I will place my hand on my heart and ride this wave of uncertainty. I am safe even if they don't reply right now. I can do this."

🍺 Alcohol & Substances

Trigger: Transition anxiety, habit loop

Body Sensations: Buzzing feeling, irritability, can't settle down

"Here is the 5 PM wave. I'm noticing how jittery my mind feels. My brain is saying, 'Just one to relax.' I'm not going to fight the thought, but I'm not going to obey it. I will breathe into this jittery feeling. Just for today, instead of numbing, I am going to do the hard thing and lean into my anxiety."

🍕 Emotional Eating

Trigger: Emotional hunger vs physical hunger

"I notice the urge to eat when I'm not hungry. This is emotional hunger, not physical hunger. My body is trying to soothe something. I'm going to pause and ask: what am I really hungry for? Connection? Comfort? Rest? I can give myself what I truly need."

🔒 Pornography & Compulsive Behaviors

Trigger: Seeking escape, dopamine, numbing

"I notice the urge for escape. My brain is seeking dopamine and numbing. This urge feels urgent, but it's just a wave. It will peak and pass. I don't need to act on it. I can sit with this discomfort. What am I really seeking? Connection? Validation? I can meet that need in a healthier way. This urge is temporary. I am in control."

Tips for Success

  • Don't fight the urge - Observe it without judgment
  • Name the sensation - "This is anxiety" or "This is craving"
  • Breathe deeply - Slow, deep breaths activate your calm response
  • Track your progress - Data helps you see patterns and celebrate wins
  • Be patient - Urge surfing is a skill that improves with practice
  • No judgment - If you give in, record it and learn from it
🎯 Remember: Every time you successfully surf an urge, you're rewiring your brain. You're proving to yourself that you can handle discomfort without acting on it. That's powerful.

When to Seek Additional Help

Urge surfing is a powerful tool, but it's not a replacement for professional help. Consider reaching out to a therapist if:

  • Urges are interfering with daily life
  • You're experiencing withdrawal symptoms
  • Urges are related to self-harm
  • You feel overwhelmed or unsafe
