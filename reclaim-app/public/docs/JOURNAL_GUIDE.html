📖 Document Your Experiences - User Guide

What is This Feature For?

This is your comprehensive evidence journal for documenting long-term experiences in a potentially narcissistic or abusive relationship. Unlike quick incident trackers (like Stonewalling or Reactive Abuse journals), this feature is designed for:

  • Extended experiences that unfold over hours, days, or weeks
  • Complex situations with multiple behaviors and patterns
  • Evidence collection with photos, audio recordings, and detailed documentation
  • Legal or therapeutic use where comprehensive records matter
  • Pattern recognition across multiple experiences over time
⚖️ Court-Admissible Documentation:
This journal creates timestamped, detailed records that can be exported for legal proceedings, custody battles, restraining orders, or therapy sessions. Every entry includes date, time, location, witnesses, safety ratings, and evidence attachments.

When to Use This vs Other Journals

Use This Journal When:

  • The incident lasted more than a few minutes
  • You have photos, audio, or video evidence to attach
  • Multiple narcissistic behaviors occurred in one incident
  • You need detailed documentation for legal/therapeutic purposes
  • The situation involved witnesses or specific locations
  • You want to track emotional impact and safety concerns

Use Quick Journals (Stonewalling, Reactive Abuse) When:

  • You need to log something quickly (under 2 minutes)
  • It's a single, specific behavior pattern
  • You're in the moment and need fast documentation
  • No evidence attachments needed

What You Can Document

📅 When & Where

Precise timestamps and locations matter for legal documentation:

  • Date & Time: Exact when the incident occurred
  • Location: Where it happened (home, public place, workplace)
  • Witnesses: Who else was present (critical for legal cases)

📝 What Happened

Detailed narrative of the incident:

  • Title: Brief summary for quick reference
  • Description: Full account of what occurred
  • Their exact words: Direct quotes (crucial for DARVO, gaslighting evidence)
  • Your response: How you reacted

🎭 Behavior Assessment

Identify specific narcissistic patterns:

  • Gaslighting
  • Projection
  • Silent Treatment
  • Love Bombing
  • Triangulation
  • DARVO (Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim & Offender)
  • Hoovering
  • Flying Monkeys

💔 Emotional Impact

Track how experiences affect you:

  • Mood Rating: 1-10 scale before and after
  • Emotions: Anxious, confused, angry, numb, scared, etc.
  • Physical symptoms: Headaches, nausea, insomnia, panic attacks
  • Long-term effects: How this impacts your daily life

🛡️ Safety Assessment

Critical for understanding escalation patterns:

  • Safety Rating: 1 (immediate danger) to 10 (completely safe)
  • Threats made: Direct or implied
  • Physical intimidation: Blocking exits, destroying property, invading space
  • Escalation indicators: Is the behavior getting worse?

📸 Evidence Collection

Attach proof to strengthen your documentation:

  • Photos: Broken items, threatening texts, injuries, living conditions
  • Audio recordings: Conversations, threats, gaslighting in action
  • Screenshots: Text messages, emails, social media posts
  • Documents: Financial records, legal papers, medical reports
⚠️ Recording Laws:
Check your local laws about recording conversations. Some states require two-party consent. However, recording in your own home for safety purposes is often legal. Consult a lawyer if you plan to use recordings in court.

Step-by-Step: Creating an Entry

Step 1: When & Where

Example:
Date: March 15, 2024
Time: 8:30 PM
Location: Our apartment, living room
Witnesses: Our daughter (age 7) was in her room but could hear

Step 2: What Happened

Title: "Accused me of cheating after I came home from work late"

Description: "I texted at 5pm that I'd be home late due to a project deadline. Got home at 8:15pm. He immediately started interrogating me about where I was, who I was with, why I 'really' stayed late. When I showed him my text from earlier, he said I was lying and probably deleted messages."

Their exact words: "You're a liar. I know you're seeing someone. You always do this. You're just like your mother - a cheater and a manipulator."

Your response: "I tried to stay calm and show him my work emails. He knocked my phone out of my hand. I went to our bedroom and locked the door."

Step 3: Behavior Assessment

Patterns identified:
• Gaslighting (denying I texted him)
• Projection (calling ME a manipulator)
• Triangulation (bringing up my mother)
• Intimidation (knocking phone from hand)

Step 4: Emotional Impact

Mood before: 7/10 (tired but okay)
Mood after: 2/10 (scared, confused, exhausted)
Emotions: Anxious, scared, angry, confused
Physical symptoms: Racing heart, shaking hands, couldn't sleep until 3am

Step 5: Safety Assessment

Safety rating: 4/10
Concerns: He's never hit me but the phone-knocking was new. He blocked the bedroom door for 10 minutes before I could lock it. Daughter heard everything.
Escalation: Yes - physical intimidation is new behavior

Step 6: Evidence

Attached:
• Screenshot of my 5pm text telling him I'd be late
• Photo of my phone (small crack from being knocked down)
• Screenshot of work email showing project deadline

Why This Documentation Matters

🧠 For Your Own Clarity

Narcissists make you doubt your reality. Written records with timestamps and evidence help you:

  • Remember what actually happened (vs their revised version)
  • See patterns you might miss in the moment
  • Validate your own experiences
  • Track escalation over time

⚖️ For Legal Proceedings

Courts need evidence, not just your word:

  • Custody battles: Pattern of behavior affecting children
  • Restraining orders: Documented threats and escalation
  • Divorce proceedings: Financial abuse, emotional abuse evidence
  • Criminal cases: Harassment, stalking, threats

🩺 For Therapy

Therapists can help you better with detailed records:

  • Identify trauma patterns
  • Develop safety plans
  • Process specific experiences
  • Track your healing progress

Export & Sharing

Every entry can be exported as a PDF with:

  • All text documentation
  • Embedded photos and evidence
  • Timestamps and metadata
  • Professional formatting for legal use
🔒 Privacy & Safety:
• Store exports in a secure location (cloud storage with strong password)
• Don't leave evidence on shared devices
• Consider using a separate email account for documentation
• If you're in danger, prioritize safety over documentation

Tips for Effective Documentation

  • Document as soon as possible: Details fade quickly
  • Be specific: "He yelled for 20 minutes" is better than "He was mean"
  • Include direct quotes: Their exact words matter for gaslighting evidence
  • Note witnesses: Even if they didn't intervene, they saw/heard it
  • Track patterns: Review old entries to see escalation
  • Rate safety honestly: This helps you see when things are getting worse
  • Attach evidence immediately: Don't wait - they might delete texts or photos

Remember

You are not crazy. You are not overreacting. If you feel the need to document your experiences this thoroughly, that itself is evidence that something is wrong. Trust your instincts. Your documentation might save your life, your children, or your sanity.

🆘 If You're in Immediate Danger:
Documentation is important, but your safety comes first. If you're in danger:
• Call 911 (or your local emergency number)
• Go to a safe location
• Contact a domestic violence hotline: 1-800-799-7233
• Tell someone you trust
You can document later when you're safe.
