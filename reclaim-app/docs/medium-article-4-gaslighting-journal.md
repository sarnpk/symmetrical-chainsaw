# I Started Recording Everything. It Saved My Custody Case.

"That never happened. You're making things up again."

I stared at my phone. I had the text message. Right there. From three days ago. Where he said exactly what he was now denying.

But somehow, I was the one questioning reality.

## When Documentation Becomes Survival

My lawyer said it during our first meeting: "Do you have proof?"

Proof of what? That he was emotionally abusive? That he gaslit me daily? That he said one thing and did another?

"Texts, emails, recordings. Anything that shows a pattern."

I had nothing. Three years of marriage, and I couldn't prove a single thing he'd done. Because emotional abuse doesn't leave bruises.

Or so I thought.

## The Moment I Started Keeping Track

It was a Tuesday. He'd promised to pick up our daughter from school. I had a work meeting I couldn't miss.

At 3:15, the school called. No one had picked her up.

I called him. "I never said I'd pick her up. You're always trying to make me look bad."

I was sure he'd said it. 90% sure. But that 10% doubt? That's what gaslighting does. It makes you question everything.

That night, I started a journal. Not a feelings journalâ€”a facts journal.

**Date. Time. What was said. What actually happened.**

No emotions. No interpretations. Just facts.

## What I Found

Within two weeks, I had documented:
- 6 promises he'd made and denied making
- 4 times he'd accused me of things he'd actually done
- 3 contradictory statements about the same event
- 2 times he'd rewritten history about our marriage

Seeing it on paper was different than remembering it. My memory could be questioned. Documentation couldn't.

## The Custody Hearing

His lawyer painted me as "unstable" and "vindictive." Said I was making up stories to keep the kids from their father.

My lawyer pulled out the journal.

**Exhibit A:** Text from him saying he'd pick up our daughter. Screenshot of school's call that he didn't show.

**Exhibit B:** Email where he said I could have the kids on his weekend. Recording of him later denying he'd ever said it.

**Exhibit C:** Six months of documented patterns showing he'd promise involvement, then blame me when he didn't follow through.

The judge's face changed. This wasn't he-said-she-said anymore. This was documented pattern of behavior.

I got primary custody.

## What I Wish I'd Known Earlier

Start documenting the moment you think something's off. Not when you're ready to leave. Not when you're in court. **Now.**

Because here's what happens: They'll deny everything. They'll say you're crazy, vindictive, making it up.

And if you don't have proof, people will believe them. Narcissists are charming. They're convincing. They're good at playing victim.

Documentation is your insurance policy.

## How to Actually Do This

I tried regular journaling. It didn't work. Too much emotion, not enough facts.

Then I found a tool specifically designed for this: Gaslighting Truth Journal.

It prompts you for:
- **What they said** (exact words)
- **What actually happened** (facts only)
- **Evidence** (screenshots, recordings, photos)
- **Contradictions** (when their story changes)

The AI even detects patterns you might miss. Like how he always gaslights on Sundays. Or how he contradicts himself within 48 hours.

## The Evidence That Matters

**Text messages:** Screenshot everything. Even the "nice" messages. Especially the nice messages. Love-bombing is part of the pattern.

**Emails:** Forward to a separate account they can't access. Print them. Back them up.

**Voice recordings:** Check your state's laws. Some require two-party consent. But if you're in a one-party state, record every conversation.

**Photos:** Timestamps matter. If they say they were somewhere, and you have a photo proving otherwise, that's evidence.

**Third-party witnesses:** School emails, doctor's notes, texts from friends who witnessed behavior.

The small stuff matters. The pattern is made of small stuff.
