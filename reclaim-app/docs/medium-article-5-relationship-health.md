# I Took a 10-Question Test. It Told Me My "Perfect" Relationship Was Toxic.

We looked perfect on Instagram.

Date nights. Couple photos. "Love you babe" comments. My friends said they were jealous.

I was miserable.

But I couldn't explain why. He wasn't hitting me. He wasn't cheating (that I knew of). He just... made me feel small. Constantly.

"You're too sensitive," he'd say. "I'm just joking. Why do you always take things so seriously?"

I started to believe him. Maybe I *was* too sensitive. Maybe this was just what relationships were like.

## The Question That Started It

My sister asked me something over coffee: "Are you happy?"

I opened my mouth to say yes. Nothing came out.

She pulled out her phone. "Take this. It's 10 questions. Just answer honestly."

It was a relationship health check. Free, anonymous, takes 2 minutes.

I almost didn't do it. What if it said everything was fine? Then I'd know the problem was me.

But I took it anyway.

## The 10 Questions That Changed Everything

I don't remember all of them, but these three hit different:

**"Does your partner respect your boundaries, or do they push until you give in?"**

I sat there for a full minute. He always pushed. Always. And I always gave in because it was "easier."

**"Do you feel like you can be yourself, or do you monitor what you say to avoid conflict?"**

Oh. I monitored everything. What I wore. What I said. Who I talked to. When I laughed. I'd been doing it so long I didn't even notice anymore.

**"After spending time with your partner, do you feel energized or drained?"**

Drained. Every single time. Even the "good" days left me exhausted.

## The Results I Didn't Want

**Relationship Health Score: 3.2/10**

**Assessment: Multiple red flags indicating emotional manipulation and control.**

**Patterns Identified:**
- Boundary violations
- Emotional exhaustion
- Self-monitoring behavior
- Isolation from support system
- Gaslighting indicators

**Recommendation: Seek support from a therapist or domestic violence advocate.**

I stared at my phone. This couldn't be right. We weren't *that* bad. He loved me. He said so all the time.

My sister read over my shoulder. "Babe. This is what I've been trying to tell you."

## The Part I Couldn't Deny

The test asked one more question at the end: "If your best friend described this relationship to you, what would you tell them?"

I'd tell them to leave.

I'd tell them they deserve better.

I'd tell them this isn't love.

But somehow, when it was my relationship, I couldn't see it.

## What Happened Next

I showed him the results. Huge mistake.

He laughed. "This is ridiculous. These tests are designed to make everyone think they're in a bad relationship. It's a scam."

Then he got serious. "I can't believe you'd take some random internet test over what we have. Do you know how much I've sacrificed for you?"

There it was. The guilt trip. The victim-playing. The deflection.

For the first time, I saw it clearly.

## Why These Tests Matter

Therapists cost money. Many of us can't afford it, especially when we're being financially controlled.

These free tests aren't perfect. They can't diagnose abuse. But they can do something crucial: **They give you permission to trust your gut.**

When you're being gaslit, you lose faith in your own perception. An objective assessment—even a simple one—can break that spell.

## The Comments I Know Are Coming

"But tests can be wrong!"

Yes. They can. But if a test says your relationship is unhealthy, and your gut agrees, maybe listen.

"This is just fear-mongering!"

20,000+ people have taken this test. The average score is 7.2/10. Most relationships are healthy. The test isn't designed to find problems—it's designed to identify patterns.

"What if my partner sees I took this?"

Use incognito mode. The test is anonymous. No email required. No data stored.

## Six Months Later

I left. It wasn't easy. It wasn't clean. But I left.

I took the test again last week. With my new partner.

**Score: 8.9/10**

The difference? I can breathe. I can be myself. I don't monitor my words. I don't walk on eggshells.

Turns out, healthy relationships exist. I just didn't know what they looked like.

## Take the Test

If you're reading this and something feels off in your relationship, take 2 minutes.

Answer honestly. Not how you want things to be. Not how they used to be. How they are right now.

If it comes back healthy, great. You have peace of mind.

If it doesn't... at least you'll know. And knowing is the first step to changing.

I wish I'd taken this test two years earlier. Would've saved me a lot of pain.

But I'm grateful I took it when I did. Because it gave me the clarity I needed to choose myself.
