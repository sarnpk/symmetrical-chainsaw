🔄 Reactive Abuse Journal Guide

What is Reactive Abuse?

Reactive abuse occurs when someone responds to being confronted about their behavior by flipping the situation and making YOU seem like the problem. Instead of addressing your concern, they turn it around so you end up defending yourself or apologizing.

Example:
You: "When you yell at me, it makes me feel scared."
Them: "YOU'RE the one who makes me yell! You're so sensitive. I'm the victim here - you're always attacking me!"

Common Patterns (DARVO)

  • Deny: "I never did that"
  • Attack: "You're crazy/too sensitive/making things up"
  • Reverse Victim & Offender: "I'M the one being hurt here"

What to Track

  • What You Addressed: The original issue you tried to discuss
  • Their Reaction: How they flipped it (victim reversal, counter-accusation, deflection)
  • What They Accused You Of: The claims they made about you
  • Did You Apologize: Track if you ended up saying sorry for bringing it up
  • Original Issue Resolved: Was your concern ever actually addressed?

Why This Matters

Over time, you'll see patterns:

  • Topics you can NEVER discuss without being blamed
  • How often you apologize for their behavior
  • Whether your concerns are ever actually heard
⚠️ Reality Check: If you can't bring up legitimate concerns without being made to feel like the abuser, that's a major red flag. Healthy relationships allow for accountability.

How to Use This Journal

  1. Document incidents when you try to address something and it gets turned around
  2. Note what you said, how they reacted, and how it made you feel
  3. Track whether you maintained your boundary or ended up apologizing
  4. Review patterns to see which topics are "forbidden"

Healthy vs. Reactive Abuse

Healthy Response:
"I hear that my yelling hurt you. I'm sorry. I was frustrated but that's not okay. Let's talk about what triggered this."
Reactive Abuse:
"How dare you say I yell! YOU'RE the one who pushes my buttons. I'm the victim of YOUR behavior. You owe ME an apology!"

Remember

Being able to express concerns without being attacked is a basic relationship right. If every attempt to discuss problems results in YOU being blamed, that's manipulation, not communication.
